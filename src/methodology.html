TRANSPARENT ON-CHAIN METRICS

How migrations are counted

Public operations, grouped consistently. These are tracker classifications, not an official declaration of a user's migration round.

One event, multiple lockups

A successful create_claimable_balance from the monitored migration source is grouped by recipient and transaction hash. Short and long lockups in the same transaction are summed into one event. A claim is a withdrawal of an existing balance, not a new migration.

First and second migration

A create_account for the same recipient in the same transaction is treated as a first-migration signal. The historical index orders distinct migration hashes per wallet: first hash, second hash, then later events.

Two or four balance operations alone do not prove the round. Transactions must belong to the same recipient and the history must be checked. A partial history can leave events unclassified or require later corrections.

Lifetime versus recent activity

Lifetime counters represent wallets with a detected first or second migration. The second group is included in the first; do not add these counts together.

Recent metrics use the period printed on each card: 24 hours, 7 days or 15 days. Daily charts show 14 UTC calendar days, including the current partial day.

Preserved lifetime totals can retain earlier higher values during an index rebuild. This prevents visible drops but does not independently validate earlier classifications or replace a backup of wallet records.

Average, median and shares

Average is total Pi divided by event count. Median is the middle event amount after sorting, or the average of the middle two amounts for an even sample. Both combine lockups before calculation.

First/second volumes, distributions and shares exclude pending and later-round events. Second migration share divides the second-round sample by the combined first-plus-second sample. Classification coverage uses all observed recent events as its denominator.

Lock periods and balances

Lockup distribution uses the duration inferred from claim predicates at creation, not time remaining. Buckets use up to 30 days, over 30 to 183 days, over 183 to 365 days, and over 365 days. Unrecognized predicates remain unknown. An elapsed lock period does not prove Pi has been claimed.

Displayed account balances are Horizon native balances and may include reserves; they are not a guarantee of spendable Pi.
